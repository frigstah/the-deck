The Deck

Four directions for the site, and the system underneath all four.

The palette, the meter and the mark are already decided — they came out of the product and they are not up for a vote. What is up for a vote is the stance: what the first screen is, and what it asks of somebody who has never heard of this. Pick one, or tell me which halves to cross.

Fixed — the same in every direction

The system

Every colour below is lifted from Theme.xaml and the dark overrides in App.xaml.cs. Not sampled by eye — copied. A site that invents its own petrol is a site that stops matching the product the first time either one moves.

The neutrals lean very slightly cool-green in both themes, which is what keeps petrol from looking like a sticker on top of grey. Red is the on-air colour and nothing else — using it for a button would spend the one signal the product cannot afford to blunt.

Three roles, and the third is not decoration: every number in the product is monospaced so digits do not shuffle sideways as they change. The site inherits that — any figure quoted on the page is set in the readout face, not the body face.

Shape and motion

  • Nothing is rounded. The product has exactly one rounded thing — the verdict pill — and that is what makes it read as a badge.
  • Hairlines, not shadows. One pixel of border is the whole vocabulary of separation.
  • The meter is real. Same segment count rule, same −60 dB floor, same 0.65 curve, same zone thresholds as the app draws.
  • Reduced motion stops it. Same argument as setup's slide: the meter settles and holds.

What I need from you

One decision, one sentence

Which direction — and the first line. Every headline above is a placeholder I wrote to fill the space. The claim about what The Deck is for is yours: who is standing at the microphone, and what were they using before.

Once those two are settled the rest is build: GitHub Pages out of this repo, real screenshots of the three sizes, the download pointed at /releases/latest so it never goes stale, and a line saying plainly that this is alpha.

On the typefaces. Everything above renders in faces that ship with Windows, because that is what the product uses and what you are looking at this on. A public site needs its own — self-hosted, not a CDN — so whichever direction wins, its display face becomes a licensing decision rather than a design one. The readout face stays a monospace stack either way; that one genuinely does not need to be exotic.

On the meters. They are not decoration standing in for the real thing. Each canvas runs DbToMeterScale — the same −60 dB floor and 0.65 exponent the app uses so the −20 to 0 region gets more width — with the same segment-count rule, the same 2-pixel gaps and the same four zone colours. The signal driving them is synthetic and speech-shaped: no audio file, no autoplay to fight, and no microphone permission asked of somebody who just clicked a link.
